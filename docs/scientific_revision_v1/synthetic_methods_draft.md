# Supplementary Methods draft: finite synthetic implementation controls

Prepared on 2026-10-16 from existing, frozen synthetic controls. This text does not describe a new analysis. Proposed supplementary Tables S11 and S12 are exact copies of the existing cohort-level results and generation checks. Their numbering remains provisional until manuscript integration.

## Purpose and execution provenance

Four prespecified data-generating processes (DGPs) were used to illustrate the behavior of the evaluated candidate-selection and representation pipelines under explicitly constructed signals. The controls were not designed to estimate the prevalence or causes of zero molecular solutions in real drug-response data, establish a general advantage of REO, or replace label-permutation controls for the eight real compounds. Ten separately generated cohorts were evaluated for each DGP, giving 40 cohorts. Each cohort contained 240 independent synthetic sample groups, eight tissue labels with 30 samples each, and 220 synthetic genes. These controls were frozen under execution protocol P5e-synthetic-v1.1 before any synthetic model fit. The original v1 configuration was retained; the v1.1 amendment fixed the support of random variates and clarified the outer/inner split seeds. The executed source commit was `d151b668f916fda643e90ba1a26035cd103b9036`, with execution-lock SHA256 `c149bb2d41d3d1a13c874e99ac73c087b25f12d4d5684c32582ff54d461043fd`.

## Data generation

Let tissue index be \(t_i\in\{0,\ldots,7\}\), \(h_i=(t_i-3.5)/3.5\), gene index \(j\in\{0,\ldots,219\}\), baseline \(b_j=3+0.008j\), and \(\ell_j\) be 220 equally spaced values from −1 to 1. Define the generated expression-scale variable as \(L_{ij}=\log_2(X_{ij}+1)\), with synthetic TPM input \(X_{ij}=2^{L_{ij}}-1\). The original implementation uses the variable name `log2_tpm`; its mathematical meaning follows this conversion and is therefore log2(TPM+1). Independent standard-normal draws used for \(\epsilon\), \(\eta\), and \(a\) were rejection-truncated to [−3,3]. Within a cohort, draws followed the frozen NumPy `Generator(PCG64(seed))` stream: arrays were generated in C row-major order and out-of-support elements were redrawn in increasing flattened-index order until all were in support. All outcomes were clipped to [0.01,0.99]; expression was not subsequently clipped or imputed.

The four DGPs were:

1. **Tissue-only signal:** \(L_{ij}=b_j+0.20h_i\ell_j+0.35\epsilon_{ij}\) and \(y_i=0.50+0.28h_i\). The outcome is exactly constant within each tissue; expression noise does not enter the outcome.
2. **Weak molecular signal:** \(L_{ij}=b_j+0.15h_i\ell_j+0.35\epsilon_{ij}\), \(z_i=\sqrt{5}\,\operatorname{mean}(\epsilon_{i0},\ldots,\epsilon_{i4})\), and \(y_i=0.50+0.05h_i+0.04z_i+0.14\eta_i\).
3. **Common sample amplitude:** \(L_{ij}=b_j+0.20a_i\) and \(y_i=0.50+0.04h_i+0.12a_i+0.02\eta_i\). Thus \(X_{ij}+1=2^{b_j}2^{0.20a_i}\): the sample-specific factor scales TPM+1, rather than TPM itself. The within-sample gene order, and hence both rank and complete pair representations, is identical across samples by construction. Generation checks recorded this exact invariant for all 220 genes; this scenario is an intentional information-removal control.
4. **Predefined pair-order signal:** initially \(L_{ij}=b_j+\epsilon_{ij}\); the first two genes were replaced with \(L_{i0}=3.5+0.35\epsilon_{i0}\) and \(L_{i1}=3.5+0.35\epsilon_{i1}\). The outcome was \(y_i=0.50+0.04h_i+0.15\operatorname{sign}(L_{i0}-L_{i1})+0.03\eta_i\). This deliberately places a response component in the ordering of two lower-variance driver genes. It is a constructed selector/representation stress case, not a representation-neutral benchmark.

Stable synthetic gene identifiers were `SIMGENE_0001` through `SIMGENE_0220`. For scenario indices \(s=0,1,2,3\) in the order above and replicate indices \(r=0,\ldots,9\), the cohort seed was `2026101400 + 100*s + r`. The exact 40 seeds, ranges, dimensions, and cohort/split file hashes are retained in Table S12. Blank invariant fields outside the common-amplitude scenario mean not applicable, not failed.

## Model comparison and nested evaluation

Eight arms were retained: a tissue-only reference, the strong expression elastic-net baseline, and two candidate-gene rules (MAD and within-training-tissue response association) crossed with continuous expression, within-sample rank, and complete three-valued pair encoding. The same training-only detection, candidate construction, scaling, unpenalized tissue projection, and relative-alpha elastic-net policies as in the development comparison were used. The matched arms selected 200 genes and encoded 200 continuous or rank features, or 19,900 pair features. Although the strong-expression budget was 2,000, only 220 genes were generated; all 220 were retained in its outer training fits. It must therefore be described as the 220-gene strong-expression arm in this control experiment. No random-forest arm was fitted.

Each cohort used one three-fold outer evaluation and three-fold inner tuning. Splits used shuffled `StratifiedGroupKFold`, stratified by tissue, with one unique biological group per synthetic sample. The outer seed was `cohort_seed + 1000`; the inner seed was `cohort_seed + 1100 + zero_based_outer_fold` on the outer-training samples. Every arm shared the cohort's splits. Each outer test fold contained 80 samples, with 160 in its training set. Feature selection and all fitted preprocessing were repeated within the relevant training partition. The ten inner candidates combined relative alpha values {1,0.3,0.1,0.03,0.01} with L1 ratios {0.5,1}; the pooled inner OOF RMSE selected the candidate, retaining the analytical zero endpoint and the frozen exact-tie rule. Each molecular outer task contained 30 inner fits and one outer refit; each tissue-reference task required one fit. The complete design consequently contained 960 outer tasks, 26,160 fit events, and 76,800 sample-by-arm OOF predictions. The ten DGP cohorts per scenario are generated cohorts, not ten repeats on a single cohort.

## Descriptive endpoints, retained undefined results, and operational records

Each cohort-by-arm row used its complete 240-sample OOF predictions to calculate Spearman correlation, RMSE, MAE, and R-squared. Molecular increments were additionally characterized using the OOF tissue reference: \(e_i=y_i-\widehat y_{i,\mathrm{tissue}}\), \(q_i=\widehat y_i-\widehat y_{i,\mathrm{tissue}}\), and Spearman correlation between \(e\) and \(q\), both pooled and within each tissue. A within-tissue correlation was undefined if either vector was constant. The strict eight-tissue mean required all eight tissue correlations to be defined; the available-tissue mean and its coverage were recorded separately. RMSE gain was tissue-reference RMSE minus model RMSE, so positive gain favors the model. No undefined correlation was replaced by zero or removed from Table S11.

Existing scenario summaries report the mean, median, minimum, maximum, and number of defined values across ten cohorts. They are descriptive; no p-values, inferential confidence intervals, power claims, or new label-permutation estimates were calculated. Selected outer-refit zero solutions are distinguished from zero coefficients among all inner-grid and outer fits: the latter denominator includes deliberately evaluated analytical-null endpoints. All warnings, fallbacks, failed-task status, and operational resources were retained in the original execution records. Execution used one isolated task process at a time and one numerical-library thread; runtime and sampled memory are operational observations, not isolated algorithm-efficiency comparisons. Actual-runtime package versions were not separately recorded in the synthetic run metadata/lock; the reproducible source is identified by its execution commit and hashes, and repository dependency pins should not be presented as a newly verified runtime snapshot.
