## Discussion

The comparison distinguishes molecular prediction from a representation-specific benefit. Both primary molecular pipelines showed descriptive development increments over the tissue reference, whereas the REO-pair pipeline did not establish an average ranking gain over strong expression in development or across screens. These findings answer different questions about the same predictors and motivate evaluating gene-order features through their contribution within a specified selection-and-learning procedure.

This distinction complements recent drug-response benchmarks. DrEval shows why tuned simple models and task-appropriate references are needed to assess added complexity [7], while the specification-game analysis demonstrates that an apparently favorable score can reflect dataset structure rather than the intended discrimination task [8]. The present analysis examines pair encoding within this evaluation problem by matching candidate genes and accounting for tissue context. The strong expression comparison tests the specified practical REO pipeline; the matched 200-gene comparisons help distinguish that pipeline comparison from a comparison of representations within a common candidate set. These are complementary questions. Neither a favorable correlation nor a gain over tissue alone identifies which part of a molecular pipeline produced the gain.

Previous REO studies motivate order-based prediction and report useful signatures in specific settings [12,13,14,15,16,17,18]. Their results do not imply that order encoding must improve every prediction task. Sparse qualitative classification signatures, patient response signatures and a complete 19,900-pair elastic-net regression differ in their targets, feature construction and learning constraints. Here, the matched-candidate comparison favored continuous expression under MAD selection, whereas the response-associated comparison did not establish an average pair advantage. The rank contrasts likewise varied descriptively with the candidate rule. Candidate selection therefore belongs in the interpretation of a representation comparison, rather than being treated as a background preprocessing choice. However, the response-associated rule selected genes using continuous expression–response associations within training tissues. It was protected from test-fold leakage but was not neutral to expression magnitude; the findings do not isolate a universal advantage or disadvantage of order information.

Including ranks helps clarify what the comparison can explain. Rank and complete-pair representations both describe within-sample gene order, while their linear predictors and elastic-net penalties impose different learning constraints. The pair expansion can change how an ordering is used by a fitted model without introducing a new measurement. Consequently, expression-to-rank and rank-to-pair contrasts are useful diagnostic comparisons, but subtracting their performance differences cannot identify causal shares attributable to amplitude loss, dimensionality, collinearity or penalization. The finite synthetic controls illustrate this distinction: a common-amplitude signal could be removed by order encoding, while a deliberately specified pair-order signal could favor a response-associated pair model. These scenarios illustrate possible behavior of the implemented pipeline under known inputs; the causes of the real-drug outcomes remain unresolved.

The fitted-model audit provides a second reason to distinguish prediction from molecular learning. The exact zero-molecular-coefficient endpoint was selected in 430 of 840 molecular outer fits and 29 of 56 final molecular models. These were legitimate outcomes of the elastic-net selection policy, not computational failures. At this endpoint, predictions retained the tissue component, so an overall correlation could remain even though the fitted model contributed no molecular term. This gives a direct explanation for the three identical external primary comparisons: both models reduced to the same tissue predictor. Conversely, nonzero coefficients alone do not establish useful molecular prediction. The zero-solution frequency describes the behavior selected by this tuning objective and regularization path; it does not prove that the underlying drug-response data contain no molecular signal.

Cross-screen evaluation asks a further question: whether a fitted predictor retains useful associations when the response-measurement setting changes. Absolute external ranking associations remained for tissue, expression and REO models, so the near-zero REO-minus-expression contrast should not be described as disappearance of all transferable signal. The descriptive external absolute means also do not by themselves establish a statistically supported molecular increment above tissue. Because both screens used the same historical CCLE expression source, this experiment concerns response transport from GDSC2 to CTRP v2, rather than robustness to a new transcriptomic platform. Previous cross-study work identifies response assays, dose windows and response summaries as relevant sources of disagreement [3,4,5,6,8]. They provide plausible context for the present findings, but this design cannot assign the observed transport limits to any single cause. Complete prediction coverage establishes that the frozen eligibility and inference rules were executable for this cohort; it is separate from evidence of predictive value.

There is also a limited connection to the evaluation of single-cell perturbation models. Simple baselines, collapsed predictions and systematic variation have become important concerns in that field [27,28,29], while calibrated metrics can reveal gains over uninformative references [30]. These studies motivate identifying the prediction target and the contribution remaining beyond an appropriate baseline. The connection is methodological: the present zero-coefficient endpoint and drug-response task differ from deep-model mode collapse and perturbed-transcriptome prediction. Applying these diagnostics to virtual-cell models would require direct evaluation in those tasks.

Several limitations define the scope of these conclusions. The eight-compound panel and comparison hypotheses were informed by earlier project analyses; the frozen evaluation is a development comparison, not an independent confirmatory study. CTRP v2 had also appeared in an earlier project manuscript, so the locked execution does not imply that this resource was wholly unseen throughout the project's history. The bootstrap intervals condition on saved predictions or fixed final models and exclude uncertainty from repeating feature selection, tuning and fitting; repeated partitions reuse biological groups. Development tissue-reference and rank contrasts are descriptive and do not share the multiplicity-adjusted status of the primary comparisons. No new eight-drug full-pipeline label-permutation experiment was performed. The finite synthetic controls are not an empirical null distribution, retained 267 warning occurrences in the common-amplitude scenario, and lack a separately recorded original runtime package snapshot. Only 34 of 80 prespecified leave-one-tissue-out contrasts were defined, leaving the strict macro undefined and preventing a complete summary of transfer to unseen tissues. Finally, the study covers eight compounds, one external screen and differing AUC dose windows. Broader claims require independently designed evaluations, and the present comparisons establish neither equivalence nor non-inferiority.

## Conclusions

In this eight-compound setting, the evaluated REO-pair models did not establish an average predictive advantage over strong expression models. Descriptive development increments above tissue were shared by molecular representations, and some fitted predictors retained only the tissue component. The study therefore distinguishes three questions that should be assessed separately: whether a model predicts response, whether molecular features add to tissue information, and whether their representation provides an additional benefit. For order-based drug-response prediction, matched candidate rules and explicit reporting of zero molecular solutions make these distinctions visible; cross-screen evaluation remains a separate test of transportability.
