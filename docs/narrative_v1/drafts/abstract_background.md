## Abstract

**Background:** Within-sample relative expression ordering (REO) is attractive because it preserves gene order under common strictly increasing transformations, but it discards expression magnitude. Whether this trade-off improves drug-response prediction must be distinguished from the contributions of tissue origin and candidate-gene selection. We compared expression, rank and gene-pair representations of matched 200-gene sets under two training-fold selection rules, alongside tissue-only and 2,000-gene expression baselines in an elastic-net pipeline. A fixed panel of eight compounds, carried forward from earlier development, underwent repeated nested cross-validation in 626 cell-line groups and retrospective transfer to 175 non-overlapping groups from the Cancer Therapeutics Response Portal, version 2 (CTRP v2). Both screens used the same Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia expression source.

**Results:** Gene-pair encoding did not establish an average ranking advantage over the strong-expression baseline. Equal-drug mean Spearman differences (pair minus strong expression) were −0.0059 in development and −0.0004 across screens, with family-adjusted conditional bootstrap intervals of −0.0187 to 0.0066 and −0.0422 to 0.0435, respectively. These intervals condition on saved predictions or fixed fitted models. Observed development increments above the tissue reference were shared by REO and expression models. Within matched gene sets, representation contrasts varied with the selection rule without establishing an average pair advantage. In 430 of 840 molecular outer fits, the selected model had zero molecular coefficients and reproduced the tissue reference.

**Conclusions:** In this eight-compound setting, molecular increments above tissue were not unique to REO, and pair encoding did not establish an average gain over expression models. Separating tissue contribution, molecular increment and representation contrast helps interpret what an order-based predictor contributes within its selection-and-learning procedure.

## Background

Transcriptomic drug-response prediction uses molecular differences between cancer models to estimate their sensitivity to treatment. Large cancer-cell-line resources and integrated pharmacogenomic databases make these associations accessible across many compounds and cellular contexts [1,2]. Yet the response being predicted is an experimental measurement: cross-study comparisons and the original drug-screening resources show that measurements arise from distinct assays, dose ranges and response summaries [3,4,5]. The scientific value of a transcriptomic predictor therefore depends on both the information extracted from expression and the drug-response task against which it is evaluated.

Choosing that task is consequential. Dose-response analysis affects how experimental measurements are summarized [6], while benchmarking studies emphasize the effects of baseline selection, evaluation design, dimensionality reduction and the intended prediction setting [7,8,9,10]. Cell-line expression models have also been used to generate predictions and biomarker hypotheses for patient-response studies [11]. For representation comparisons, these applications motivate a specific question: whether changing expression encoding adds predictive value beyond tissue structure and an appropriate expression-based reference.

Relative expression ordering (REO) offers a qualitative way to encode expression: a pair records which of two genes is more highly expressed within a sample. Qualitative transcriptional signatures have been investigated for treatment-response prediction [12,13], and order-based methods have been developed to analyze expression across datasets and transcriptome contexts [14,15]. Related applications include resistance-associated expression analyses, relative-rank approaches to compositional data, and categorical chemotherapy-response signatures [16,17,18]. Their rationale is relevant to representation choice: applying the same strictly increasing transformation to every gene in a sample preserves its gene order. This property provides a reason to investigate order-based prediction, but discarding expression magnitude can also remove response-associated variation. Within-sample rank vectors and complete pair encodings both describe gene order, but define different linear predictors and regularization geometries. A complete-pair model can therefore behave differently from either a rank model or a continuous-expression model without gaining new measurement information.

Candidate-gene selection and tissue composition complicate this comparison. Selecting genes changes the molecular information available to every representation, and an outcome-associated selection rule need not favor continuous and order-based models equally. Tissue effects can also contribute to an overall prediction score even when no molecular coefficients are retained. These considerations call for comparisons that distinguish the tissue reference, the increment from molecular features, and the difference between molecular representations. Outcome-informed selection must also remain within training partitions: work on biological machine learning, limited-sample validation, nested feature selection and reproducibility explains why information flow and the independent evaluation unit matter [19,20,21,22,23]. Such safeguards make a representation comparison interpretable; they do not themselves establish a predictive benefit.

Here, we asked whether REO improved continuous drug-response ranking in a fixed eight-compound panel carried forward from earlier development. The primary comparison evaluated a response-associated gene-pair model against a strong 2,000-gene expression baseline. Matched 200-gene comparisons crossed two training-only candidate rules with continuous expression, within-sample rank and complete-pair representations under the same tissue-adjusted learning procedure. We examined both performance above a tissue-only reference and whether the fitted models retained a molecular contribution. Repeated nested cross-validation characterized development performance, while tissue-held-out analysis and a locked retrospective transfer from GDSC2 to the Cancer Therapeutics Response Portal, version 2 (CTRP v2), examined additional evaluation contexts. The cross-screen comparison kept the historical expression source fixed while changing the drug-response screen.

## Editorial rationale and evidence boundaries

- This is a prose-only draft dated 21 October 2026. It does not replace the author reading copy, alter the analysis plan, or add analyses. No individual CTRP response, prediction or fitted model was accessed for this draft.
- The abstract has three BMC sections. Its numerical claims are copied from existing M08/author-review aggregates, with display rounding only: development primary Δρ = −0.005882, interval [−0.018714, +0.006614]; external primary Δρ = −0.00037164, interval [−0.042166, +0.043505]; zero molecular fits = 430/840. The intervals remain conditional, not full-pipeline refitting uncertainty.
- The statement about development increments refers to the existing descriptive pair-minus-tissue and strong-expression-minus-tissue contrasts. It is not an assertion of a new adjusted inferential result or an external molecular increment.
- The abstract identifies the panel as carried forward from earlier development. The full Methods must retain its outcome-informed status and explain that the external workflow was locked although CTRP had appeared earlier in the project's history.
- The introductory sequence is resource and prediction task → evaluation question → REO rationale and trade-off → matching candidate selection and tissue references → the specific study. Run counts, folds, parameter grids and warnings remain in Methods/Results rather than serving as introductory motivation.
- References 1–23 retain their original identities and first-appearance order. References 24–31 belong to the unchanged Methods, Discussion and availability sections; none is deleted or renumbered here. The draft introduces no new literature or claims that a new source has been independently verified in this writing pass.
- REO invariance is limited to a common strictly increasing within-sample transformation. It does not establish invariance to gene-specific bias or demonstrate cross-platform robustness. Rank and pair encodings both describe within-set order; no exact invertibility claim about tied-value encodings is needed for this narrative, and their linear hypothesis classes and penalties differ.
- Differences across candidate rules are descriptive here. Neither a causal allocation of losses to amplitude/dimensionality/penalization nor a new formal policy-by-representation interaction is claimed.
- Zero molecular coefficients are valid selected solutions, not solver failures or evidence that the real biological system lacks a molecular signal. The study does not establish equivalence, non-inferiority, or the failure of all REO methods.
