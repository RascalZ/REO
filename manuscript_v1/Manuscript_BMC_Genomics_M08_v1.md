## Abstract

**Background:** The incremental value of within-sample relative expression ordering (REO) for continuous drug-response prediction remains uncertain. We compared a tissue reference, a strong 2,000-gene expression model, and matched 200-gene expression, rank, and full-pair models under two training-fold candidate rules. Eight fixed compounds and 626 development cell-line groups underwent five-fold outer cross-validation repeated three times with three-fold inner tuning. Full-development models underwent one locked retrospective CTRPv2 evaluation in 175 non-overlapping groups. Four finite synthetic controls examined specified implementation behavior.

**Results:** In development, the predefined REO-pair minus strong-expression difference in equal-drug mean Spearman correlation was −0.00588 (family-9 adjusted conditional bootstrap interval, −0.01871 to 0.00661). The RMSE improvement, strong expression minus pair, was −0.00075 (nominal conditional 95% interval, −0.00124 to −0.00028), favoring expression. In the locked CTRPv2 evaluation, equal-drug mean correlations were 0.2284 for tissue, 0.2602 for strong expression, and 0.2599 for REO pair; the primary difference was −0.000372 (family-9 adjusted interval, −0.04217 to 0.04350; 1,185 drug–group responses). Matched-budget comparisons did not establish an average pair advantage. Zero molecular coefficients occurred in 430 of 840 outer fits and 29 of 56 final molecular arms. The strict leave-one-tissue-out macro was undefined: only 34 of 80 prespecified contrasts had defined correlations. Synthetic results illustrated tissue-only prediction, weak-signal shrinkage, order-invariant amplitude loss, and a constructed pair-order signal; they did not identify real-data mechanisms.

**Conclusions:** The evaluated REO pipeline did not show a reliable average ranking improvement over strong expression in this eight-compound setting. This does not establish equivalence or non-inferiority or exclude other REO formulations. Reporting tissue contribution, candidate policy, representation contrasts, zero molecular solutions, and assay context is necessary to delimit such comparisons.

## Background

Large cancer-cell-line pharmacogenomic resources have enabled systematic comparisons between molecular profiles and drug-response phenotypes [1,2,3,4,5]. These screens provide useful experimental systems for studying response-associated patterns, but their dose ranges, assay procedures, and response summaries are not interchangeable. Cross-study analyses have documented that performance can change when models move between screens, while recent benchmarking studies emphasize evaluation design, response metrics, and comparisons with simple baselines [3,6,7,8,9,10]. Accordingly, a model that predicts a cell-line screen outcome should be interpreted as a predictor of that experimental endpoint unless tested against an independent biological or clinical outcome [11].

Within-sample relative expression ordering (REO) represents the sign of the expression difference between two genes in the same sample. Because a common strictly monotone transformation preserves order, pairwise order can be less sensitive than absolute values to some sample-wide scale changes. REO-based methods and qualitative signatures have been applied to differential-expression inference and treatment-response or resistance classification [12,13,14,15,16,17,18]. Those studies motivate evaluation of order-based representations, but their settings include differential-expression tasks and patient-level categorical signatures; they do not establish that pair features improve continuous drug-response prediction in cell lines. In addition, encoding all pairs among 200 genes produces up to 19,900 features and discards expression magnitude. The resulting model may therefore behave differently from either a 200-dimensional rank model or a continuous-expression model, even though a rank vector and its complete pair expansion encode the same within-sample ordering.

High-dimensional biological prediction can be biased when outcome-informed feature selection, preprocessing, or tuning uses information from held-out observations. Guidance and empirical studies recommend nesting all learned steps within training partitions, respecting related sample identities, reporting uncertainty appropriate to the independent unit, and making the information flow auditable [19,20,21,22,23]. Nested validation alone does not establish useful generalization: finite-sample variability, tissue composition, endpoint definition, model complexity, and the choice of baseline also matter [7,8,9,10,21]. These concerns are especially relevant when feature selection itself is response-associated. Prior benchmarks already motivate careful baselines and explicit prediction targets [7,8]. Our narrower contribution is a matched candidate-policy-by-representation comparison under the same tissue-adjusted learning rule, with audits of when a nominal molecular predictor reduces to its covariate reference.

Here, we evaluated whether a response-associated full gene-pair model added drug-ranking information beyond a strong 2,000-gene expression baseline in a fixed panel of eight compounds. We also compared expression, within-sample rank, and complete pair encodings within the same 200-gene candidate sets selected by either a training-only variability rule or a training-only response-association rule. A tissue-only reference was used to describe the contribution of tissue means; repeated nested cross-validation, leave-one-tissue-out analysis, zero-solution auditing, and one locked GDSC2-to-CTRPv2 cross-screen evaluation were used to characterize the scope and limitations of the comparisons. The study was designed as a comparative evaluation of a specified learning pipeline, not as a search for the best-performing drug subset or as a clinical prediction study.

## Methods

### Study design and estimands

This was a retrospective computational comparison of continuous drug-response prediction models in cancer cell lines. The primary question was whether a training-fold response-associated gene-pair model improved drug-wise response ranking over a strong expression baseline across a fixed panel of eight compounds. Secondary questions compared expression, within-sample rank, and full pair encodings within matched 200-gene candidate sets; quantified performance relative to a tissue-only reference; described zero molecular solutions and tissue-held-out behavior; and evaluated transfer of fixed models from GDSC2 to CTRPv2.

The eight-compound panel was carried forward from the project's identity-, coverage-, and dose-metadata-frozen panel. The panel was not altered according to results of the current repeated evaluation or CTRPv2 evaluation. However, the formulation of the candidate-selection and representation hypotheses was informed by earlier project analyses. Accordingly, the current repeated evaluation is a prespecified, outcome-informed development comparison, not an independent confirmatory study. The CTRPv2 evaluation was locked before its response values were opened by the new evaluation workflow and executed once; CTRPv2 had nevertheless appeared in an earlier manuscript, so the evaluation is not prospectively blind in the history of the project.

### Data sources, expression measurements, and compound mapping

Drug-response measurements for model development were taken from the frozen GDSC2 fitted dose-response release dated 27 October 2023 [5]. Gene expression was taken from the historical Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) RSEM gene TPM matrix dated 29 September 2018 [1]. The expression matrix contained 1,019 models and 57,820 measured gene records before the project-specific identity and annotation mapping. The analysis used 19,836 uniquely mapped protein-coding stable Ensembl gene identifiers. Gene-expression values were used as TPM; no raw-count differential-expression analysis or DESeq2 step was part of the reconstructed pipeline. DepMap 24Q4 Model/README and Cell Model Passports metadata snapshots were used for identity and tissue mapping only [24,25]; their expression data were not mixed into the 2018 CCLE matrix. CTRPv2 response records used only in the locked external evaluation derive from the original CTRP screen [4].

Expression gene records were annotated against the CCLE-matched gencode.v19 reference using complete versioned Ensembl identifiers: 56,238 of 57,820 source records matched. Only 19,836 explicitly protein-coding records entered modeling. The terminal numeric version was removed for stable feature identifiers without collapsing or averaging records; no duplicate resulting identifiers were found. Unmatched records were annotation-unresolved, not assigned a noncoding biotype. The reference filename, checksum, and annotation checks are in Additional file 14. The local source audit did not re-download the complete historical TPM matrix to establish byte-for-byte remote identity.

Eight compounds were fixed by their GDSC2 identifiers: MIRA-1 (1931), PLX-4720 (1036), Bortezomib (1191), Telomerase Inhibitor IX (1930), Tamoxifen (1199), BMS-536924 (1091), Gefitinib (1010), and Cediranib (1922). Their CTRPv2 compound identifiers and chemical-identity evidence are reported in Additional file 8 (Table S8). Name and structure cross-references, including Standard InChIKey matches where available, supported the compound mapping; this metadata match does not chemically authenticate the material used in either assay. GDSC2 and CTRPv2 concentration ranges differed and were not harmonized post hoc.

### Response outcome and analysis units

The continuous outcome was the source-reported fitted dose-response area under the curve (AUC), analyzed on the source AUC scale without dichotomization or response thresholds. Training-only target standardization was inverted for evaluation. Under the GDSC documentation's viability-curve convention, a larger AUC corresponds to greater residual viability across tested concentrations and thus lower in-vitro drug sensitivity [26]. The analysis unit was a canonical cell-line biological group for one compound. Related or duplicate model identities were grouped before data splitting so that a biological group could not occur in both training and test partitions. Each eligible group contributed at most one response record per compound after the frozen cohort curation rules.

### Cohort curation and eligibility

Cell-line identities were reconciled by authoritative exact CCLE, Broad/DepMap, Sanger, and COSMIC cross-references; ambiguous identities were not resolved by name similarity. Common biological groups used Broad same-tissue-sample PatientID, Sanger patient links, and known parent/derivative relationships. Broad PatientID does not establish a complete patient genealogy. The cancer-focused cohort-v2 rules excluded explicit Non-Cancerous annotations and quarantined missing or Unknown disease annotations unless exact-ID cancer provenance was established. One exception, HCC1588 (ACH-001078; CVCL_A351; SIDM01820), was retained with concordant cancer-cell-line evidence. This metadata amendment followed a development-only pilot and preceded external response access by the reconstructed workflow. Tissue names were canonicalized by exact case-insensitive vocabulary matching.

The primary CTRPv2 cohort required SNP-matched-reference identity status and excluded all known GDSC2 and related biological groups, including candidate identities in unresolved mappings, rather than only groups observed for the eight drugs. A separate list of unconfirmed/untested SNP identities does not constitute an evaluated sensitivity cohort or evidence of misidentification. The fixed panel met cohort-v2 feasibility minima of 200 development and 80 primary external groups per drug; these are not power guarantees. The original cohort-v1 minimum was 50 external groups with a preferred 80-group pool, from which all eight were selected. Cohort-v2 yielded 626 development groups and 4,967 drug–group responses, and 175 external groups and 1,185 responses after prespecified within-compound/group median aggregation. Counts are in Table 1; exact identity and exclusion rules, historical coverage ordering, and per-compound dose-window selection are in Additional file 14.

### Candidate-gene selection and molecular representations

Every learned operation, including detection filtering, candidate selection, representation construction, scaling, tissue adjustment, regularization-path calculation, and hyperparameter selection, was repeated using only the training rows of the relevant inner fit. The outer test fold was used once for evaluation and did not contribute to feature selection or tuning.

Within each training fit, a gene was considered detected if its raw TPM was at least 1 in at least 10% of training samples. The median-absolute-deviation (MAD) rule selected genes with non-zero MAD in log2(TPM+1), ordered by decreasing MAD and then stable Ensembl identifier for ties. The response-association rule first centered log2(TPM+1) and the continuous AUC outcome within each training tissue. Among detected genes with non-zero within-tissue expression residual variance, genes were ranked by the absolute Pearson correlation between their tissue-centered expression and tissue-centered response. Stable Ensembl identifiers broke exact ties. No second MAD ranking was applied. If the training response was exactly constant within every tissue, all eligible genes received a score of zero and were ordered by stable identifier. Both candidate rules selected up to 200 genes for the matched-budget comparisons. The strong-expression baseline selected up to 2,000 genes by the same training-only detection and MAD policy.

For each selected 200-gene set, three representations were evaluated. The expression representation used log2(TPM+1). The rank representation assigned each sample average midranks among the selected genes, using the raw TPM ordering and exact ties. The full pair representation included every unordered pair of selected genes, with orientation determined by ascending stable Ensembl identifier; each feature was +1 when the first gene's raw TPM exceeded the second, −1 when it was lower, and 0 for an exact tie. At 200 genes this yields at most 19,900 pair features. Constant and high-tie pairs were retained. The rank vector and complete pair vector encode the same within-set order information, but their linear predictors and elastic-net penalties differ because the pair encoding expands that information into a larger, correlated feature space.

All molecular inputs had to be complete, finite, non-negative float64 TPM values. No missing-value imputation or post hoc clipping was used. A structural expression-schema mismatch was treated as a hard failure; a sample with invalid molecular TPM was not silently imputed. No such molecular abstentions occurred in the locked external evaluation.

### Prediction models and hyperparameter selection

Eight model arms were evaluated: (1) a tissue-only reference, which predicted the mean training AUC for the observed tissue and the overall training mean for an unseen tissue; (2) a strong expression elastic-net model using up to 2,000 MAD-selected genes; and (3–8) the two candidate-selection rules crossed with the three matched 200-gene representations.

For each molecular arm, features were constructed before standardization and tissue adjustment. A training-only standard scaler was fit to molecular features, and the current training outcome was standardized. Tissue one-hot effects were unpenalized. The molecular design and outcome were projected onto the orthogonal complement of the training tissue design by Frisch–Waugh–Lovell residualization; an elastic net without intercept was fit to these residuals. Prediction restored the training target mean and scale after combining the molecular and tissue components. For an unseen tissue the one-hot vector was zero: prediction was the training overall mean plus any retained molecular contribution, not a zero AUC. With zero molecular coefficients, this matched the tissue reference's observed-tissue mean/unseen-tissue overall-mean policy. The prediction identity is given in Additional file 14.

The elastic-net grid contained five alpha ratios (1, 0.3, 0.1, 0.03, and 0.01) crossed with L1 ratios 0.5 and 1. For each individual training fit, alpha_max was recomputed from that fit's tissue-residualized features and outcome as max|Zrᵀyr|/(n × L1 ratio); the candidate alpha was the selected ratio multiplied by alpha_max. Models used cyclic coordinate descent with a maximum of 4,000 iterations and tolerance 0.001. Hyperparameters were selected by the lowest pooled inner out-of-fold RMSE on the original AUC scale; exact ties were resolved by the first candidate in the frozen grid order. The ratio-1 endpoint was the analytic zero-molecular-coefficient solution and was retained as a legitimate candidate. Zero molecular coefficients were not considered a fit failure: the tissue component remained in the prediction. Predictions were not clipped to the [0,1] interval.

### Repeated nested cross-validation

Development used five outer folds repeated three times, with three inner folds. StratifiedGroupKFold(shuffle=True) used tissue strata and biological-group identities on the canonical-model-sorted 626-group table. Outer seeds were 20261013, 20261014, and 20261015; inner seeds were repeat_seed + 100 + zero-based outer fold. The global assignments were intersected with each compound's eligible records and shared by all arms. Group separation and exactly-once validation coverage were checked. Rare strata of one or two groups produced three outer and fifteen inner split-preparation warnings; frozen partitions were retained without score-based exclusions or reassignment. These are distinct from solver warnings. Candidate selection, feature construction, scaling, tissue adjustment, and alpha_max were rebuilt in every inner-training fit. The lowest pooled inner-OOF RMSE selected the hyperparameters, followed by one refit on all outer-training data and one held-out evaluation. The tissue reference required one fit and no tuning.

This design generated 960 outer tasks (eight compounds × eight model arms × 15 outer folds), 26,160 successful fit events, and 119,208 outer-fold predictions. Repeats reused the same biological groups under different partitions and were not treated as additional independent samples.

### Full-development models and locked CTRPv2 evaluation

After the development comparison, 64 full-development models were trained with a shared three-fold StratifiedGroupKFold assignment on the sorted 626-group table (seed 2026101501), intersected with each drug's records. One rare-stratum warning was recorded. The same grid, training-only feature pipeline, and pooled inner-OOF RMSE objective were retained. The 1,744 fit events included inner candidates and all-development refits. Artifacts were reloaded in an independent process and checked against locked source and prediction audits. Response-free CTRPv2 predictions were generated and locked before the reconstructed evaluation workflow accessed external response values. Structural schema mismatches were hard failures; invalid individual molecular inputs triggered abstention rather than imputation. Exact schema, missingness, denominator, and abort rules are in Additional file 14.

The single locked external evaluation matched predictions to CTRPv2 responses by compound and canonical cell-line group. For each compound, repeated CTRPv2 curve records for the same canonical group were collapsed by their median, yielding 1,185 valid drug–group responses from 1,201 source curve records. External comparisons were restricted to common groups for the two models in each paired contrast. The primary estimand was the within-drug Spearman correlation difference, response-association pair minus strong expression; the eight-drug macro was the strict equal-weight mean over the fixed eight compounds. A strict macro was not recalculated after omitting undefined drugs. All 9,480 planned compound-by-model predictions were finite and covered, with no abstentions. This analysis evaluates retrospective within-drug ranking transport from GDSC2 response labels to CTRPv2 response labels using the same CCLE expression source; it is neither cross-expression-platform validation nor clinical validation.

### Leave-one-tissue-out sensitivity analysis

Each of the 23 metadata-defined tissues was held out in turn. For each held-out tissue, all groups from that tissue were excluded from model development; the same drug panel, model arms, training-only feature pipeline, three-fold inner tuning, and RMSE selection rule were used on the remaining tissues. The held-out tissue was treated as unseen by the model. The primary display grid comprised the ten tissues with at least 20 development groups for every compound, yielding 80 prespecified drug-by-tissue contrasts. A drug-specific strict mean required a defined contrast in all ten tissues, and the strict eight-drug macro required a defined strict mean for all eight drugs. Undefined correlations were preserved as NA; no available-cell mean was substituted for the strict macro. This was a sensitivity analysis without a new inferential family or confidence intervals.

Held-out tissue names were sorted by Python Unicode order and indexed from zero; three-fold inner tuning used seed 2026101401 + tissue_index on each drug's remaining groups. Across 184 drug–tissue contexts, all emitted rare-stratum warnings (176 with one group; eight with two); group separation and validation coverage were verified. Before fitting, execution amendment v1.1 resolved a prose/config seed conflict in favor of the frozen machine value 2026101401. Subsequent logging and row-identity repairs produced completed execution v1.3 without changing models, seeds, grid, estimands, or scoring. The supplementary methods preserve the failed/repaired execution history.

### Performance metrics and statistical uncertainty

For each drug, model, and repeat, Spearman correlation, RMSE, mean absolute error, and R² were calculated from the complete outer out-of-fold predictions on the original response scale. The three repeat-specific metric values were then averaged. Predictions were not averaged across repeats before ranking, and the repeated predictions were not concatenated to inflate the sample size. Drug-macro values were equal-weight averages over all eight drugs; if any prespecified drug or repeat component was undefined, the strict macro was reported as NA with the reason retained.

The primary inferential family comprised nine Spearman contrasts: the primary REO-pair minus strong-expression contrast for each of eight drugs and the strict equal-drug macro. A separate 18-estimand family comprised pair-minus-expression contrasts within the MAD and response-association candidate rules, again for each drug and the equal-drug macro. Development intervals were percentile bootstrap intervals based on 10,000 shared resamples of the 626 biological groups, with the same group multiplicities applied across drugs, arms, and repeats. The family-adjusted intervals used Bonferroni-adjusted two-sided percentile limits at nominal family alpha 0.05. These intervals are conditional on the recorded out-of-fold predictions; they do not include uncertainty from repeating feature selection, tuning, or model fitting and are not guaranteed full-algorithm cross-validation coverage. The inferential families are adjusted separately and do not provide simultaneous coverage over every analysis in the study. Other model contrasts and performance metrics are descriptive with unadjusted conditional intervals.

For the external evaluation, 20,000 shared bootstrap resamples were drawn from the 175 external biological groups. The same group weights were used across all drugs, arms, and paired contrasts. The primary family remained nine estimands and the matched-candidate representation family remained 18. Each of the 27 prespecified primary and matched pair-minus-expression contrast estimands had 20,000 valid draws. External intervals are conditional on the fitted full-development models and the observed external groups; they do not incorporate model-refitting uncertainty. Zero intervals caused by two identical zero-molecular predictions are algebraic identities and do not constitute evidence of equivalence or non-inferiority. No non-inferiority margin was specified, and no equivalence or non-inferiority test was performed. No new full-pipeline permutation test was run in this eight-drug analysis.

Development used shared integer group-multiplicity draws from Generator(PCG64), seed 2026101602; tied midranks were recomputed under multiplicities, requiring total multiplicity at least two and nonconstant weighted ranks. External resampling used seed 2026101502 and required at least three distinct represented groups and nonconstant response/prediction for each correlation. Intervals required a defined point and at least 99% valid draws; no invalid draw was replaced. Percentiles used NumPy quantile(method='linear'), with adjusted tail probability 0.05/(2K). LOTO used a separate descriptive helper, requiring at least two held-out samples and nonconstant response and prediction. No undefined component was replaced by zero or omitted from a strict macro. Additional file 14 specifies the full conditional estimands and provenance limits.

Additional files 9 and 10 consolidate existing absolute external metrics and matched representation contrasts. They add no resampling or inferential family. Existing absolute-rho intervals are available for five molecular arms at the drug level only. Development rank contrasts retain existing nominal, unadjusted descriptive intervals; external rank contrasts and absolute external macros have no precomputed intervals. Missing intervals remain NA, including tissue-reference and rank-arm absolute intervals. Their absence does not mean zero uncertainty.

### Finite synthetic implementation controls

Four prespecified data-generating processes—tissue-only outcome, weak molecular signal, common sample amplitude, and predefined pair order—were each evaluated in ten separately generated cohorts. Each cohort had 240 independent synthetic groups, eight tissues of 30 groups, and 220 genes. The common-amplitude design held all within-sample orders identical; the pair-order design deliberately assigned signal to two lower-variance driver genes. The same eight arms used three outer folds and three inner folds, with training-only preprocessing and the same relative-alpha grid. Matched arms used 200 genes, while the nominal 2,000-gene strong arm actually used all 220 available genes. All 40 cohorts were retained. Cohort metrics and generation checks are in Tables S11 and S12; equations, RNG rules, execution provenance, and all scenario results are in Additional file 14. These were descriptive controls without inferential intervals, not a representation-neutral benchmark, real-data mechanism identification, or a substitute for full-pipeline real-label permutation. Actual synthetic runtime package versions were not separately recorded.

### REO-pair versus strong-expression performance in development

The primary equal-drug macro Spearman correlation was 0.2931 for the response-association pair model and 0.2990 for the strong 2,000-gene expression model. The prespecified difference, pair minus strong expression, was −0.00588 (family-9 Bonferroni-adjusted conditional interval, −0.01871 to 0.00661). Six of eight drug-specific point estimates were negative and two were positive; every drug-specific family-adjusted interval included zero. The macro difference was negative in all three repeated partitions (−0.00393, −0.00655, and −0.00716), which describes partition-level direction consistency but does not create three independent replications. The paired drug-wise and equal-drug estimates are shown in Figure 2.

For the macro RMSE, the strong-expression model had 0.06217 and the REO-pair model had 0.06292. The paired improvement, defined as strong-expression RMSE minus pair RMSE, was −0.00075 (unadjusted conditional 95% interval, −0.00124 to −0.00028), favoring the strong-expression model on this error measure. The tissue-only macro Spearman was 0.2366; the descriptive response-association pair minus tissue-only difference was +0.05654 (unadjusted conditional interval, +0.04299 to +0.07004). The strong-expression minus tissue-only difference was +0.06242 (+0.04926 to +0.07577). These tissue-reference contrasts indicate that both molecular modeling pipelines can add ranking information over the tissue reference in this development evaluation, but they do not establish an REO-specific advantage. Drug-level results and all absolute metrics are reported in Table 2 and Additional files 3–5 (Tables S3–S5).

### Matched-candidate representation comparisons

Within the MAD-selected 200-gene set, the equal-drug pair-minus-expression difference was −0.01765 (family-18 adjusted conditional interval, −0.03117 to −0.00406). Within the response-association-selected 200-gene set, the corresponding difference was −0.00225 (−0.01438 to +0.00949). Thus, in the fixed comparisons, full pair encoding did not outperform continuous expression on average within the same candidate-gene budget. The response-association pair-minus-expression interval included both negative and positive values; it does not establish equality or non-inferiority. The rank representation, all drug-specific estimates, and the other candidate-by-representation contrasts are retained in Additional files 3–5 (Tables S3–S5). The matched-candidate contrasts are summarized in Figure 3A.

The response-association rule produced a descriptive macro difference of +0.05654 over the tissue-only reference, while the strong-expression model differed from the same reference by +0.06242. These results are consistent with molecular predictions adding ranking signal beyond tissue means in some parts of the evaluation, but the average increment was not specific to pair encoding. Candidate selection based on response-associated continuous expression was performed within training folds, yet the contrast remains conditional on the selected outcome-informed workflow.

The rank arm further distinguishes order encoding from the complete pair expansion (Table S10). Under MAD selection, rank minus expression was −0.01244 (existing nominal conditional 95% interval, −0.01879 to −0.00622), and pair minus rank was −0.00521 (−0.01222 to +0.00181). Under response-associated selection, rank minus expression was +0.00441 (−0.00150 to +0.01030), and pair minus rank was −0.00666 (−0.01456 to +0.00103). These rank-related intervals are unadjusted descriptive summaries outside the two adjusted inferential families. They do not identify causal shares of amplitude loss, dimensional expansion, collinearity, or penalization.

### Zero molecular solutions and fitted-model status

Among 840 molecular outer-fold fits, 430 returned the exact zero-molecular-coefficient solution. These fits were valid outputs of the prespecified elastic-net path, not failed computations. Across the 56 molecular full-development models, 29 had zero molecular coefficients. The resulting predictions retained the tissue component; they must not be interpreted as evidence that the model learned a molecular resistance signature. The remaining models had non-zero coefficients, which alone does not establish predictive utility or biological mechanism. No solver warning or fallback was recorded in the repeated development evaluation. The per-drug and per-arm audit is reported in Additional file 6 (Table S6). Figure 3B shows the molecular coefficient status of the full-development fits.

Among the 80 prespecified drug-by-tissue cells in the ten-tissue display grid, 34 had a defined within-tissue Spearman difference for response-association pair minus strong expression. Only Telomerase Inhibitor IX and BMS-536924 had defined strict means across all ten tissues (−0.0124 and −0.0526, respectively). Because the other six drugs did not have fully defined ten-tissue means, the prespecified strict eight-drug macro was NA. We did not replace it with a mean over the 34 available cells. The defined and undefined cells are shown in Figure 4A. Full results for all 23 held-out tissues, including undefined correlations and model status, are in Additional files 1 and 7 (Tables S1 and S7).

The full-development response-association pair model had an equal-drug external Spearman correlation difference of −0.000372 relative to the strong-expression model (family-9 adjusted conditional interval, −0.04217 to +0.04350). Drug-specific point differences were heterogeneous: −0.0237 for Telomerase Inhibitor IX, +0.0303 for BMS-536924, −0.0372 for Gefitinib, +0.0270 for Cediranib, +0.0006 for Bortezomib, and exactly zero for MIRA-1, PLX-4720, and Tamoxifen. Every non-identical drug-specific family-adjusted interval included zero; the strict macro interval also included zero. The three exact zero differences occurred because both full-development models had zero molecular coefficients for those compounds, so their predictions were identical under the frozen implementation. These are not equivalence or non-inferiority results.

Within the same 200-gene candidate set, the external MAD pair-minus-expression macro difference was +0.00782 (family-18 adjusted conditional interval, −0.01884 to +0.03450), and the response-association pair-minus-expression macro difference was −0.00671 (−0.02562 to +0.01369). These absolute macros have no precomputed intervals, and the point difference from tissue is not a new inferential molecular-increment result. These are descriptive strict macro points without precomputed intervals (Table S10). Together with the adjusted pair-minus-expression results, they do not establish general pair superiority, equivalence, or non-inferiority.

## Discussion

In the prespecified eight-compound comparison, the response-associated REO-pair pipeline did not show a reliable average improvement in drug-wise ranking over the strong expression baseline in either repeated nested development evaluation or the locked CTRPv2 evaluation. The internal Spearman difference was close to zero and the internal RMSE contrast slightly favored expression; the external macro ranking difference was also close to zero with an interval that allowed both positive and negative effects. This is a result about the evaluated candidate rules, elastic-net path, response endpoint, compounds, and cell-line cohorts. It neither establishes equivalence or non-inferiority nor proves that other REO models cannot be useful. The result is consistent with recent benchmarking work showing that complexity or feature transformation does not guarantee improvement over well-defined simpler predictors [7,8,9,10].

Earlier REO studies provide a rationale for evaluating within-sample order and have reported useful qualitative signatures in particular data settings [12,13,14,15,16,17,18]. Their reported tasks and estimands differ from the present continuous in-vitro response-ranking task, so their performance values are not direct comparators. In particular, sparse top-scoring-pair classification and patient ex-vivo response signatures [18] should not be treated as the same model as a complete 19,900-pair elastic-net regression. In the present matched-candidate analyses, the full pair encoding did not outperform continuous expression on average. The response-association candidate rule was based on continuous expression–response association within training tissues; it was nested inside each training fit, but it does not constitute a selector neutral to expression magnitude. Thus, the results do not isolate a universal “order-only” effect. Moreover, rank vectors and complete pair vectors carry the same ordering information but define different linear-elastic-net hypothesis classes and penalty geometry. The observed differences cannot be attributed uniquely to lost amplitude, expanded dimensionality, collinearity, or regularization.

Tissue reference performance and molecular zero solutions further constrain interpretation. Both the response-associated pair pipeline and the strong-expression model improved on the tissue-only reference in the development evaluation, but the difference between those molecular models did not favor REO on average. Approximately half of the outer-fold molecular fits and more than half of the final molecular model arms selected the exact zero-coefficient endpoint. In those fits, prediction retains the tissue component and the associated correlation does not demonstrate that a molecular pattern was learned. The three exact-zero external primary differences are an especially clear example: both compared models had zero molecular coefficients, so identical predictions yielded a zero contrast by construction.

The cross-screen evaluation is best interpreted as retrospective within-drug ranking transport from GDSC2 responses to CTRPv2 responses under a shared CCLE expression source. Cross-study analyses and recent evaluation frameworks show that assay protocols, dose ranges, response summaries, and aggregation choices can affect agreement and model transport [3,4,5,6,8]. The current evaluation does not test transfer to a different expression-measurement platform, absolute calibration between source AUC values, prospective prediction, or patient outcome. CTRPv2 had been used in an earlier project manuscript, so the present locked execution should not be described as wholly blind in the project's history. The zero-prediction-abstention coverage is complete for the locked eligible set, but complete coverage alone does not imply strong predictive value.

Recent single-cell perturbation benchmarks highlight the need for task-appropriate baselines and explicit evaluation targets. Simple linear baselines can match or exceed evaluated deep-learning models in some settings [27]. PerturBench examines collapsed predictions [28], while Systema addresses systematic variation in perturbation-prediction evaluation [29]. Gains over uninformative baselines have also been reported using calibrated metrics [30]. Our drug-response analysis provides a complementary, task-specific case study: descriptive representation contrasts varied with the candidate-gene policy, and approximately half of the outer-fold molecular fits selected the zero-coefficient endpoint. These observations motivate reporting covariate-reference performance, molecular increments, representation contrasts, and whether fitted predictions retain a molecular contribution. Selection of an exact zero solution in the present elastic-net pipeline depends on the tuning objective and regularization path and should not be equated with mode collapse in deep perturbation models. The synthetic controls demonstrate behavior under specified mechanisms without identifying the causes of zero solutions in real drug data. Extending these checks to single-cell foundation models requires direct, task-specific evaluation.

Several limitations affect the strength and scope of inference. First, although supervised feature selection, representation construction, scaling, tissue adjustment, and tuning were repeated inside training partitions, the eight-compound panel and comparison hypotheses were informed by earlier project analyses. The present run is therefore a prespecified development comparison rather than an independent confirmatory study. Second, the bootstrap intervals condition on saved outer-fold predictions or fixed final models and do not include uncertainty from repeating the entire feature-selection, tuning, and fitting procedure. The repeated partitions reuse the same biological groups. Third, this eight-drug analysis did not include a new full-pipeline label-permutation experiment; the reported finite synthetic controls illustrate software behavior under intentionally specified scenarios but do not provide an empirical null distribution or identify the mechanisms of these drug outcomes. The common-amplitude control retained 267 warning occurrences, and the original synthetic runtime package snapshot was not separately logged. Fourth, the leave-one-tissue-out strict macro was undefined because many prespecified within-tissue correlations were not estimable; the defined-cell average cannot replace the planned macro. Finally, only eight compounds and one external screen were studied, and the AUC dose windows differed across screens. Larger and independently designed screen comparisons would be needed to test broader generality.

## Conclusions

In this fixed eight-compound cell-line study, the evaluated REO-pair pipeline did not show a reliable average improvement in drug-response ranking over the strong expression baseline in repeated nested development evaluation or the locked retrospective CTRPv2 cross-screen comparison. The matched representation analyses do not establish a pair-specific advantage, and frequent zero molecular solutions show that some fitted predictors reduce to their tissue component. These findings delimit the performance of the specified candidate-selection rules, elastic-net models, response endpoint, compounds, and cohorts; they do not establish equivalence or non-inferiority and should not be generalized to all REO methods or clinical prediction.
